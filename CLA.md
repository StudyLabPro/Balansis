# Balansis Contributor License Agreement Policy

Balansis uses a dual-licensing model:

- open-source distribution under `AGPL-3.0`
- proprietary commercial distribution under separate commercial terms

To keep that model legally workable, contributions must be accepted on terms
that allow the maintainers to publish and relicense contributions across both
licensing tracks.

## Contribution requirement

By opening a pull request, submitting a patch, sending code by email, or
otherwise contributing material to the Balansis repository, you represent and
agree that:

1. You are legally entitled to submit the contribution.
2. The contribution is your original work, or you have sufficient rights to
   submit it under these terms.
3. You grant the Balansis maintainers and copyright holders a perpetual,
   worldwide, non-exclusive, irrevocable, royalty-free license to use,
   reproduce, modify, prepare derivative works of, publicly display, publicly
   perform, sublicense, and distribute your contribution:
   - under `AGPL-3.0`;
   - under the Balansis commercial license;
   - and under future license updates for the Balansis project, provided such
     relicensing is performed by the maintainers or copyright holders.
4. You retain copyright in your own contribution except to the extent rights
   are expressly licensed above.
5. You understand that the maintainers may refuse or remove contributions that
   cannot be cleanly dual-licensed.

## Corporate contributions

If you contribute on behalf of an employer, client, university, or other
organization, you confirm that the organization has authorized the submission
and that the organization agrees to these terms.

For larger or recurring commercial contributions, the maintainers may request a
separate signed individual or corporate CLA before merge.

## Inbound equals maintainable outbound

The project applies an "inbound rights sufficient for outbound dual licensing"
policy. In practice, this means maintainers must receive enough rights to ship
accepted contributions in both the open-source and commercial editions.
